This policy explains how Deep Learning Blocks (deeplearningblocks.com) processes personal data. It follows the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and, where it applies, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

In short

  • You don’t need an account. There are no ads and no cookies.
  • Your boards, drawings, trained models and progress stay in your browser. They are never uploaded.
  • The playground sends a small set of usage events to PostHog in the EU. They help us see which lessons work. They contain no names, drawings or board contents, and no identifier that survives a reload.
  • Our host, Cloudflare, processes technical request data to deliver the site.

2. Data stored on your device

The playground saves your canvases, unlocked levels, completed lessons and experiment notebook in your browser’s local storage (key dlb-playground-v4). This data never leaves your device. We cannot see it. Digits you draw and all training run locally in your browser. Trained models are kept only in memory and disappear when you reload.

This storage is needed for a feature you use, so it doesn’t require consent. To delete it, use Fresh paper in the playground for a single canvas, or clear this site’s data in your browser settings.

3. Hosting and delivery (Cloudflare)

The site is served by Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. To deliver pages and protect the site from attacks, Cloudflare processes technical data with every request: your IP address, date and time, the requested URL, your browser’s user agent and the referring page. We don’t run our own servers and don’t keep our own access logs.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in delivering a secure, working website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Cloudflare may process data in its global network, including the USA. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Frameworks. It also processes data under a data processing agreement with Standard Contractual Clauses. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

4. Usage statistics (PostHog)

The playground (/play) uses PostHog, a product analytics service by PostHog Inc. Our project is hosted on PostHog’s EU cloud in Frankfurt, Germany. The introduction and these legal pages load no analytics.

We send only these events, with only the listed details:

  • Page view of the playground, with the address but no query string or fragment.
  • A workspace or level was opened, or a level was completed (mode, level and dataset).
  • Training started, completed, was stopped or failed (network size, test accuracy, duration). Error messages are not sent.
  • Python code was downloaded (file format and network size).

We have deliberately configured PostHog to:

  • store nothing on your device (no cookies, no local storage),
  • use a random ID kept only in memory, so a reload starts a new one and we can’t recognise returning visitors,
  • create no personal profiles,
  • record no clicks, keystrokes, sessions or screen recordings, and drop referrers and other browser details,
  • not look up your location from your IP address.

To send these events, your browser connects to PostHog, which receives your IP address as part of the network request. We use the statistics to improve the lessons and to fix problems. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in understanding how the learning tool is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). PostHog processes the data for us under a data processing agreement. Because PostHog Inc. is based in the USA, access from there is possible. Such transfers are protected by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, with the amendments required for Switzerland. Events are kept for the retention period of our PostHog plan, currently up to one year. See PostHog’s privacy policy.

Opting out: If your browser sends a “Do Not Track” signal, the playground loads no analytics at all. Content blockers also stop the events. The playground works the same either way.
